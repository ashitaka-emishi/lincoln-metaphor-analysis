# Corpus Selection Criteria

V4 selection expands coverage while preserving auditability. It does not replace close reading with scale, and it does not treat larger size as a cure for interpretive judgment. Every added document must have a documented reason for its tier assignment.

## Shared Eligibility Criteria

A document can enter any v4 tier only when the project can record:

- stable title, date or date range, and date precision;
- source edition or source authority;
- source URL or archive reference where available;
- authorship status and authorship confidence;
- register, genre, audience, and rhetorical function;
- known source, transcription, edition, manuscript, or collaboration risks;
- relationship to existing v1 documents and research questions.

Documents with uncertain authorship or unstable text can still be useful, but their tier and evidentiary limits must make the uncertainty visible.

## Tier 1 Selection Criteria

Tier 1 additions should be high-value documents that justify full annotation. Selection should prioritize documents that:

- fill an underrepresented period, register, audience, or rhetorical situation;
- test or complicate an existing metaphor cluster or absence finding;
- improve coverage of major public arguments, wartime decisions, or reconciliation logic;
- have source quality strong enough for sentence-level annotation;
- can be segmented without destabilizing existing v1 sentence IDs;
- are important enough that full Stage 4-style annotation effort is warranted.

Tier 1 should preserve the 28 v1 documents and add 20 priority documents. The resulting 48-document corpus is an expanded interpretive core, not a comprehensive Lincoln archive.

## Tier 2 Selection Criteria

Tier 2 should broaden validation coverage beyond the core. It should include 75-100 documents that collectively improve balance across:

- chronology;
- genre and register;
- public, semi-public, legal, administrative, and private contexts;
- campaign, presidential, wartime, emancipation, constitutional, and reconstruction-adjacent rhetoric;
- high-confidence and caution-flagged source traditions.

Tier 2 is designed to test selection-bias risk and boundary conditions. It can identify whether core-corpus findings appear concentrated, missing, or unstable in a broader corpus, but it should not be cited as fully annotated interpretive evidence unless individual records have been promoted through the required gates.

## Tier 3 Selection Criteria

Tier 3 should include searchable reference material useful for discovery and context. It may include documents that are too numerous, too peripheral, too textually unstable, or too lightly vetted for immediate annotation.

Tier 3 supports:

- candidate discovery for future Tier 1 or Tier 2 promotion;
- vocabulary and phrase searches;
- negative-search audits;
- source-tradition comparison;
- documentation of why certain materials remain outside the interpreted corpus.

Tier 3 search results cannot be counted as validated metaphor instances or as absence findings.

## Selection-Bias Boundary

The v4 architecture reduces selection-bias risk by making selection criteria explicit and by separating interpretive, validation, and search-only roles. It does not eliminate bias. Selection still reflects scholarly judgment about research relevance, source quality, historical importance, and annotation feasibility.

Publication claims must therefore name the tier that supports them. V4 does not claim to analyze all Lincoln writings.
